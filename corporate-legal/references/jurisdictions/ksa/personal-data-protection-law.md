## Scope

The Law applies to any processing of personal data of individuals carried out in the Kingdom by any means, and to processing by any entity outside the Kingdom of personal data of individuals resident in the Kingdom (Art. 2). It binds controllers (جهة التحكم: whoever determines purpose and means) and, through them, processors (جهة المعالجة: whoever processes on the controller's behalf) (Art. 1). For a commercial contract review it supplies: the mandatory content of a controller-processor agreement (Art. 8, Implementing Regulation Art. 17), the lawful bases including consent and legitimate interest (Art. 5, 6, 10, 15), the cross-border transfer conditions (Art. 29), breach notification (Art. 20, Regulation Art. 24), DPO and records duties (Art. 30, 31, Regulation Art. 32, 33), and the penalty ceilings (Art. 35, 36). It does not cover purely personal or family use (Art. 2(2)); it does not displace more protective rules in other laws (Art. 3), the National Cybersecurity Authority's remit (Royal Decree M/19 item Fourth), or SAMA's supervisory powers (Art. 30(1)). Data-subject rights (Art. 4, Regulation Art. 3-8) are noted in one row only; the privacy plugin holds the fuller treatment.

## Open questions for a local practitioner

- Edition of the Implementing Regulation: the SDAIA English file has no edition date or decision number in its front matter; its Art. 1 cites M/148 (as both the 2023 and 2024 editions do). The content matches the post-M/148 regulation (legitimate-interest article, 72-hour breach rule, three DPO cases, five-year record retention). Confirm whether the file is the September 2024 amended edition and whether any provision relied on above (Art. 11, 16, 17, 24, 25, 32, 33) changed in that amendment.
- The Regulation on Personal Data Transfer Outside the Kingdom was not read: confirm the current list of permitted "other purposes" (Art. 29(1)(d)), the adequacy list, the SDAIA Standard Contractual Clauses (whether mandatory in form), Binding Common Rules, certification, the transfer-risk-assessment trigger, and the exemption and withdrawal procedure under Art. 29(4).
- Whether SDAIA has issued rules under Regulation Art. 34 requiring specific controllers to register in the National Register of Controllers, and under Regulation Art. 32(4) on DPO appointment, and whether either applies to foreign processors.
- Whether the Competent Authority has issued rules or guidance treating processor-to-controller breach notice as subject to a fixed deadline (the Regulation says "without undue delay").
- Whether SDAIA has issued a mechanism under amended Art. 33(4) requiring foreign entities to appoint a local representative or register, replacing the repealed Art. 33(2).
- Formal status of the Competent Authority after the two-year period in Council of Ministers Resolution 98 item Second (SDAIA versus the National Data Management Office), and the identity of the enforcement committee under Art. 36(2).
- Interaction with the E-Commerce Law Art. 5 (consent for disclosure of consumer data) and with SAMA and CST sector rules for regulated controllers (Resolution 98 items Sixth and Seventh contemplate memoranda of understanding, not read).
- Arabic original of the Implementing Regulation was not read; the SDAIA English was used and is an authority document, not the Bureau of Experts translation.
