## Scope

Part VIII imposes on every employer a set of workplace safety duties (hygiene, hazard information, personal protective equipment, fire precautions), a matching duty on the worker to use protective means, a special regime for major-hazard establishments, and a compensation regime for work injuries and occupational diseases that applies only where the establishment is not covered by the occupational-hazards branch of the Social Insurance Law (GOSI). It also requires first-aid provision, annual medical examinations for workers exposed to occupational diseases, preventive and curative health care within the cooperative health insurance framework, and welfare provision at remote sites. The Regulations add the work-injury reporting procedure for non-GOSI establishments, the first-aid inventory, the definition of remote locations, and the model work-regulation clauses on health insurance and GOSI registration. The 2025 Regulations extend the safety and work-injury rules to casual, seasonal and temporary workers, trainees and part-timers.

This file does not cover the GOSI occupational-hazards benefits themselves (`social-insurance` reference), the Minister's technical safety standards under Art. 121, the major-hazards regulations under Art. 131, the hazardous-professions list under Art. 131 bis, or the occupational-fitness examination regulation of 1447H — none of which were provided.

## Open questions for a local practitioner

- Which Minister's decisions currently set the Art. 121 technical standards, the Art. 131 major-hazard regulations and the Art. 131 bis hazardous-professions list, and whether any of them restricts hiring by gender or age beyond Reg. Arts. 32–35.
- The occupational-fitness examinations regulation (Ministerial Decision 33232 of 11/3/1447H) listed on the HRSD page: scope (which professions require pre-employment or periodic fitness examinations), and its interaction with Art. 143 and with Annex 5 cl. 12.5.
- GOSI occupational-hazards branch: reporting deadline, benefit levels and how liability is allocated where a worker should have been registered but was not (does the employer owe Arts. 133–138 amounts, GOSI-equivalent benefits, or both).
- Whether Art. 138 compensation is cumulative with the end-of-service award and with Art. 77 damages where the Art. 137 termination is contested.
- Time limit for Art. 135 relapse claims after termination, given the 12-month limitation in Art. 234.
- Current schedule amounts for safety violations (posting, PPE, first-aid cabinet, missed injury report) and whether closure has been applied in practice.
