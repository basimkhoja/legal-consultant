## Scope

The Law governs the commercial courts (المحاكم التجارية) and the commercial circuits established in general courts where no commercial court exists (Decree ¶2), and applies, together with its Regulations, to the court and the cases within its jurisdiction, without prejudice to the commercial laws and the international conventions the Kingdom is party to (Art. 2). It covers subject-matter and territorial jurisdiction, pre-filing notice and conciliation, electronic filing and service, written pleadings and hearing management, urgent (interim) requests, evidence in commercial cases, judgments and immediate enforcement, payment orders, appeal, reconsideration and cassation, and (in the Regulations) small claims and class actions. It does not itself govern the compulsory enforcement of judgments or the recognition and enforcement of foreign judgments and arbitral awards: those are matters for the Enforcement Law (`enforcement-law.md`) and the Arbitration Law (`arbitration-law.md`); this file only cross-references them. Where the Law is silent, the Law of Civil Procedure (نظام المرافعات الشرعية) applies insofar as compatible with the commercial nature of the case (Art. 93). Labor, administrative and criminal matters are outside the commercial courts.

## Open questions for a local practitioner

- **Art. 16(2) threshold.** The Law says SAR 100,000 (unchanged by Royal Decree M/191, which amended only paragraph (3)); the draft Regulations (Art. 49) say SAR 500,000; the article lets the Supreme Judicial Council raise the figure. Confirm whether the Council has raised it and which figure the approved Regulations carry before advising a non-merchant claimant on forum.
- **Art. 16(3) after M/191.** The draft Regulations (Arts. 50, 101(b)) still speak of mudaraba; confirm how the approved Regulations treat partnership-contract disputes under the Civil Transactions Law (written-contract requirement, mandatory conciliation, single-judge allocation).
- **Status of the Regulations text.** The Ministry of Justice file read is headed "مشروع" and dated 1441/9H. Confirm the approved Implementing Regulations (instrument number and date), and check each regulation row cited above against the approved text, especially: mandatory-conciliation list (Regs Art. 101), lawyer-filing and lawyer-pleading thresholds (Regs Arts. 94–96), single-judge thresholds (Regs Art. 17), small-claims definition (Regs Art. 322), the 5-year contract-address rule (Regs Art. 76), the travel-ban security (Regs Art. 174), the 180/20/90-day maxima (Regs Art. 69), and the 10-day judgment-copy delivery (Regs Art. 191) versus the Law's 20 days.
- **Supreme Judicial Council decisions** referred to in Arts. 33, 78, 80 (single-judge circuits, non-appealable small claims, appeals without hearing) were not read; the SAR 50,000 figure in Art. 78(1) is a ceiling "as determined by the Council".
- **Expert rules** under Art. 56 (Minister's rules on expert evidence) and the separate **Judicial Costs Law** (court fees) were not read; costs recovery in practice (lawyers' fees as damages, Regs Arts. 182–183) is reported to be conservative. `[model knowledge — verify]`
- **International jurisdiction** rules (Art. 15 → Law of Civil Procedure) and the treatment of foreign forum-selection clauses by the commercial court are not covered here; practice on dismissing in favour of a foreign court is unsettled. `[model knowledge — verify]`
- **Interaction with the 2026 Enforcement Law**: precautionary attachment under Art. 36 still draws its substantive rules from the 1433H Enforcement Law chapter kept alive by Decree ¶6 to the new law until moved into the Law of Civil Procedure; which text applies to a commercial-court attachment after 2026-10-28 needs confirmation.
- **Arbitration-clause plea**: Art. 74(4) makes the ruling on the plea appealable before the merits; whether the commercial court treats a defective or pathological clause as a ground to retain the case, and how the 30-day objection waiver in Arbitration Law Art. 7 interacts, is practice-dependent. `[model knowledge — verify]`
