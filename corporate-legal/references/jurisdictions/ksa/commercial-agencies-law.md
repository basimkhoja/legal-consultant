## Scope

The Law reserves the activity of "commercial agent" in the Kingdom to Saudi natural persons and to wholly Saudi-owned companies with Saudi boards and Saudi signatories (Art. 1), requires every commercial agent to be entered in a Commercial Agencies Register at the Ministry of Commerce (Art. 3), and punishes violations of the Law and its Regulations with fines of SAR 5,000-50,000, publication of the penalty and, for foreigners and companies with non-Saudi partners, administrative liquidation of the agency business, trading bans and deportation (Art. 4 as amended in 1400H). The 1400H additions make the statute apply to anyone who contracts with a producer (or the producer's substitute in its country) to carry on commercial business "whether as agent or distributor in any form of agency or distribution", so a Saudi distributor of a foreign principal is inside the Law by its own words, not only by regulation, and is subject to statutory duties to secure spare parts and maintenance during the agency and for a year after it ends (also extended to importers and professional resellers). The Law does not regulate exclusivity, termination or compensation; those points, the registration timing and the documentary requirements are in the Implementing Regulations and Ministry practice, which were not in the sources for this file. The Franchise Law (2019) and the Anti-Concealment Law are the neighbouring instruments `[model knowledge — verify]`.

## Open questions for a local practitioner

- **Nationality relaxation.** Art. 1 is unamended on the portal and still says non-Saudis cannot be agents. Confirm the exact instrument (ministerial decision amending the Implementing Regulations, or platform rule) by which MISA-licensed foreign-invested companies are now registered, and whether any residual Saudi-ownership or Saudi-signatory requirement survives.
- **Three-month registration window and late filing.** Confirm the current Regulations article, the consequence of late registration (fine under amended Art. 4, refusal, or acceptance), and whether the Ministry registers distribution agreements for services or only for a producer's goods (Added Art. 1 speaks of "المنتج").
- **Validity inter partes.** Confirm current Commercial Court practice on enforcing unregistered distribution agreements, in particular whether a non-registered Saudi distributor can be met with a plea of illegality when it sues the principal.
- **Model contract status.** Whether the Ministry still requires the contract to follow its model (Added Art. 4 says "for guidance") as a condition of registration on the electronic platform.
- **Spare-parts duty in practice.** How the Regulations define "spare parts consumers usually demand", the "reasonable period" for others, and how the one-year post-termination duty is enforced against a distributor whose principal has cut off supply.
- **New law.** Whether the Ministry of Commerce's proposed replacement Commercial Agencies Law (circulated for consultation in recent years) has been issued and whether it introduces termination compensation or changes registration.
- **Franchise boundary.** Which distribution structures the Ministry treats as franchises under the Franchise Law (with its own disclosure and registration regime) rather than as agencies.

## How skills should use this file

- `vendor-agreement-review` (for distribution, reseller and agency agreements with a Saudi counterparty): at the "regulatory compliance" step, cite Art. 1 (nationality rule as written), Art. 3 (registration), Added Art. 1 (distributors are inside the Law) and Added Art. 2 (statutory spare-parts and maintenance duty, including the one-year post-termination tail) and flag that the Saudi party must register the agreement; at the "termination" step, cite the termination row (no statutory compensation, `[model knowledge — verify]`) and check that run-off terms cover the Added Art. 2 tail; at the "exclusivity" step, cite the exclusivity row. Findings that rest on the Regulations or on court practice must carry `[model knowledge — verify]` and recommend counsel confirmation.
- `saas-msa-review`: rarely relevant; if a Saudi "reseller" or "channel partner" will resell a SaaS subscription for commission, flag that Added Art. 1 covers distribution of a producer's products "in any form" and that whether services resale is caught was not resolved from the text; stop and refer to the open question.
- `escalation-flagger`: escalate any structure where a foreign principal controls a Saudi agent (Anti-Concealment row), any agency with a non-Saudi shareholder that has not been confirmed as permitted under the relaxed Regulations, and any consumer-durables distributorship with no spare-parts/maintenance allocation (Added Art. 2).
- `diligence-issue-extraction`, `tabular-review` and `material-contract-schedule`: for each distribution/agency agreement extract principal, products, territory, term, exclusivity, whether a Ministry registration certificate exists for the current term (Art. 3 register contents row), and whether after-sales obligations are allocated (Added Art. 2). Record missing registration as a regulatory-breach finding against the agent (amended Art. 4: SAR 5,000-50,000 plus publication), not as a contract-validity finding; tag the validity point `[model knowledge — verify]`.
- `closing-checklist`: where the target is a registered agent, add "update the Commercial Agencies Register for change of control / change of signatories" (Art. 1 requires Saudi board and signatories for an agency company as written), "obtain principal consents", and "confirm spare-parts and maintenance continuity for one year after any terminated agency" (Added Art. 2) as closing items, tagged `[model knowledge — verify]` for the platform mechanics.
- Stop rule: the settled rows are Arts. 1-6 and the 1389H/1400H additions. If a skill needs the three-month deadline, the consequences of non-registration between the parties, termination compensation practice, the nationality relaxation or the model-contract requirement, it must state that the Implementing Regulations were not read, cite the relevant open question, and stop or flag for Saudi counsel rather than fill the gap from memory.
