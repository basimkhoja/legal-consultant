## Scope

The Law binds "government entities": ministries, government agencies, authorities, public institutions and bodies with independent public legal personality (Art. 1; Art. 10), including their works and purchases executed outside the Kingdom (Art. 11), and it also binds companies that carry out works and procurement *on behalf of* government entities (Art. 93). It does not, on its own terms, bind state-owned or PIF-owned commercial companies contracting for their own account: stc, Saudi Aramco, SABIC, Ma'aden, SEC and similar companies are not "government entities" under Art. 1, the Law treats companies at least 51% state-owned as *contractors* (they are exempt from the performance bond, Art. 61(3)(د)), and such companies run their own procurement policies unless their articles or a specific decision apply the GTPL `[model knowledge — verify]`; confirm this for each counterparty, because some authorities and public institutions with commercial names (for example a "company" wholly owned by a ministry and acting on its behalf) fall under Art. 93. The Law governs the government side of the relationship: how the contract is awarded, what bonds and penalties the government may take, when it may vary, withdraw, or terminate, and where the contractor complains. A private subcontractor of a GTPL contractor is bound by the Law only where the contractor's contract and the subcontract pass the terms down (Art. 71), except that subcontracting itself needs the government entity's written consent and the contractor stays jointly liable. Leasing and investment of government real estate remain under Arts. 61-63 of the 2006 law and Chapter 19 of its regulations until a separate law issues (Royal Decree para. 2).

## Open questions for a local practitioner

- **Implementing Regulations.** Not read. Confirm the current consolidated text (Minister of Finance Decision No. 1242 of 1441H as amended), and specifically: the delayed-portion computation rule for Art. 72 penalties and its article number; the advance-payment cap and recovery mechanics under Art. 66; payment timelines under Art. 67; the designated materials for Art. 68(1) price adjustment; the arbitration threshold and seat rules under Art. 92; and any retention-money provisions.
- **State-owned companies.** Confirm, per counterparty, whether stc, SEC, Saudi Aramco, SABIC, Ma'aden, the PIF giga-project companies and the ministry-owned development companies are outside the Law (Art. 1) or inside it as agents of a government entity (Art. 93), and whether any of their bylaws or a Council of Ministers decision applies the GTPL to them.
- **Convenience termination compensation.** Art. 77 is silent on compensation. Confirm what the model contracts and the Regulations grant (work done, materials on site, demobilisation) and the Administrative Court's practice on lost profit.
- **Prolongation costs.** Art. 74 gives time; confirm whether the Regulations or court practice allow a contractor to recover delay costs for employer-caused delay.
- **Change of control.** Whether a change of control of the contractor is treated as an "assignment" needing consent under Art. 70, either in the Regulations or in the model contracts.
- **Committee-to-court route.** Whether grievance-committee decisions under Art. 86 (which "bind the government entity") can be challenged by the bidder before the Administrative Court, and within what period.
- **Local-content figures.** The preference percentages, the mandatory list and the shortfall penalties under Art. 96(3) and the Local Content Authority's rules were not read.

## How skills should use this file

- `vendor-agreement-review` and `saas-msa-review`: when the counterparty is a government entity, load this file at the "counterparty type" step and treat the government's terms as largely non-negotiable (Arts. 55, 91); cite Art. 72 (delay caps), Art. 74 (extension grounds), Arts. 76-78 (termination and bond forfeiture), Arts. 70-71 (assignment and subcontracting consents), Art. 65 (tax-inclusive price) and Art. 92 (Administrative Court, limited arbitration). When the counterparty is a GTPL prime contractor and the user is its subcontractor, use the "Which rules bind whom" row: check the subcontract for back-to-back delay caps, extension grounds and termination rights and flag any gap. When the counterparty is stc or another state-owned company, cite the Art. 1 row, state that the GTPL is presumptively NOT applicable, attach `[model knowledge — verify]`, and review the counterparty's own terms.
- `escalation-flagger`: escalate any government contract with delay penalties above the Art. 72 caps (which needs Ministerial approval disclosed at tender), any foreign-seated arbitration clause with a government entity (Art. 92), any proposed assignment or subcontract without written consent (Arts. 70-71), and any counterparty on the Ministry's published ban list (Arts. 13(1)(د), 88).
- `diligence-issue-extraction` and `tabular-review`: for each government contract extract procuring entity and whether it is a "government entity" (Art. 1) or an Art. 93 agent; award method and value (Arts. 30, 32 thresholds); performance bond status (Art. 61); variations issued against the +10%/-20% limits (Art. 69); penalties applied against the 6%/20% caps (Art. 72); any Art. 75 withdrawal or Art. 76 notice; consents held for subcontracts (Art. 71); pending grievances or Art. 88 referrals; performance-evaluation results (Art. 79).
- `material-contract-schedule`: identify government contracts separately, note the Art. 70 assignment consent (entity plus Ministry) as a required consent for any transfer, and note that a change of control may be treated as assignment `[model knowledge — verify]`.
- `closing-checklist`: for a target holding government contracts add: Art. 70 consents for any assignment; confirmation that no Art. 76 mandatory-termination trigger exists (including any bankruptcy application by the target, Art. 76(1)(ب)); bond replacement on any change of the guarantor bank; Etimad account and classification transfer; ban-list check.
- Computed numbers: delay penalty cap = 6% × contract value (supply) or 20% × contract value (other) (Art. 72); variation ceiling = +10% / -20% × contract value (Art. 69); bid bond = 1-2% × bid value (Art. 41); performance bond = 5% × contract value (Art. 61); grievance clock = 5 working days (pre-award) or standstill (award) → 15 working days → 3 days → 15 (+15) working days (Art. 87). Each computed figure must carry the row's `[settled — last confirmed 2026-09-04]` tag; any figure computed on the delayed portion only must carry `[model knowledge — verify]` because that rule is in the unread Regulations.
- Stop rule: if a skill needs the Regulations (delayed-portion computation, advance-payment cap, payment timing, arbitration threshold, convenience-termination compensation, local-content percentages) or needs to decide whether a specific state-owned company is bound, it must say the source was not read or the point is tagged `[model knowledge — verify]`, cite the open question, and stop or flag for Saudi counsel. It must not fill the gap from memory.
