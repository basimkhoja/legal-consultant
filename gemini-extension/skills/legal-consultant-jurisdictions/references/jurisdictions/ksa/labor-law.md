## Scope

The Labor Law governs every contract under which a person works for an employer, under the employer's management or supervision, for a wage (Art. 5), including government and public-body workers and charitable-institution workers to the extent stated, training contracts, and part-time workers only as regards occupational safety, work injuries and what the Minister decides. It does **not** apply (Art. 7 as amended by M/44) to family members who are the establishment's only workers, sports players and coaches, domestic workers and the like, agricultural workers and private herdsmen and the like, and non-Saudis entering to perform a specific task for no more than two months; those categories are governed by separate HRSD regulations, breaches of which are nevertheless punished under Art. 229. Casual, seasonal and temporary workers get only the duties/discipline, hours, rest, overtime, holiday, safety and injury provisions (Art. 6). Employment in the public sector is governed by civil-service rules, and social-insurance entitlements (pensions, occupational-hazard branch) by the Social Insurance Law, which this file does not cover. The labor dispute forum is now the Labor Courts (Part 14 of the Law was repealed by M/1 of 1435H); this file records only the limitation period and the standstill rule that survive in Arts. 234-235.

## Open questions for a local practitioner

- **Working hours discrepancy.** The Bureau of Experts English appendix lists M/46 amendments to Arts. 98, 99, 100 (9 hours / 45 hours; 7 / 35 in Ramadan) and 104 (two paid rest days including Friday), but the portal does not mark those articles as amended and the Arabic body reads 8 / 48, 6 / 36 and a single Friday rest day. This file follows the Arabic. Counsel should confirm whether those provisions were ever enacted (the writer believes they were not) and whether any ministerial decision under Art. 99 changes the position for particular sectors.
- **Art. 77 fractional years.** The compensation for terminating an indefinite contract without a legitimate reason is "fifteen days' wage for each year of the worker's service". The text does not say whether partial years are pro-rated (as Art. 84 expressly does for the award) or ignored. Court practice is needed before a skill computes a number for a worker with, say, 3 years and 7 months.
- **Art. 77 contractual compensation below the statutory formula.** Art. 77 opens "unless the contract includes a specific compensation". Whether a contractual figure lower than 15 days per year or lower than the two-month floor survives Art. 8 (conditions less favourable than the Law are void) is not settled by the text.
- **Which wage for Art. 77.** Art. 77 says "wage" (actual wage, Art. 2) but does not say "last wage" as Art. 84 does. Whether courts use the last wage or an average is a practice point.
- **Resignation from an indefinite contract and the Art. 85 fractions.** M/44 defines "resignation" as the written wish to terminate a **fixed-term** contract, and Art. 74 now lists resignation (3 bis) separately from termination at will in indefinite contracts (3). Whether a worker who terminates an indefinite contract on 30 days' notice under Art. 75(1)(a) still receives only the Art. 85 fraction of the end-of-service award, or the full award, is unresolved on the text; pre-M/44 practice applied the fractions to any worker-initiated termination.
- **Art. 79 bis and Art. 77(2).** The defined "resignation" is "without any conditions or restrictions" and the resigning worker "is entitled to all rights under this Law". Whether an employer can still claim Art. 77(2) compensation (wages for the remaining term) from a fixed-term worker who resigns and is deemed accepted by silence needs confirmation.
- **Art. 75 and "legitimate reason".** The Law does not define سبب مشروع. Whether redundancy, restructuring, poor performance without prior warnings, or loss of a client contract are accepted as legitimate reasons, and whether an employer terminating on 60 days' notice with a stated reason is still exposed to Art. 77 if the court rejects the reason, is court practice.
- **Art. 61(5)-(6) allowances.** No amount or benchmark for "appropriate" housing and transport allowances is given, and it is not stated whether an all-inclusive salary already covers them. Whether these allowances form part of the wage for end-of-service and compensation purposes (the text says they are paid "with the wage") is a practice point.
- **Retirement age.** Art. 74(4) now refers to "the retirement age under the Social Insurance Law". The Social Insurance Law and its 2024 amendments were not supplied; the applicable age (and any transitional schedule) must be taken from that instrument.
- **Art. 11 joint liability.** M/46 removed the joint-and-several liability of principal and contractor. Whether courts nevertheless reach the principal (for example under civil-law principles or the Regulations on assignment) is a practice point relevant to outsourcing reviews.
- **Non-Saudi transfer after 12 months (Regs Art. 14, item 21).** The Regulations allow a non-Saudi to move to another employer after 12 months from entry on 90 days' notice "with due regard to Art. 77". How Art. 77(2) compensation is applied in that case, and whether Nitaqat conditions restrict it, needs confirmation.
- **Dispute route details.** The mandatory amicable-settlement step, the Labor Courts' procedural rules, and the enforceability of arbitration clauses after the repeal of Art. 224 are outside the text supplied.
- **GOSI and work injuries.** Arts. 133-139 apply only where the GOSI occupational-hazards branch does not (Art. 132). Which categories of workers fall outside GOSI coverage should be confirmed before applying the Art. 137-138 amounts.
- **Domestic workers and the Art. 7 categories.** The separate HRSD regulations for domestic workers, agricultural workers and short-visit non-Saudis were not supplied; hiring reviews for those categories cannot rely on this file.
- **Regulations not read.** HRSD implementing provisions on Arts. 84-88 (award), 90 (wage-protection mechanics and exemptions), 109-111 (leave accounting), 151 (maternity documentation) and 74-77 do not appear in the April 2025 Implementing Regulations read for this file; ministerial decisions on those points (including the Wage Protection System decision and the violations-and-penalties schedules) were not supplied.

## How skills should use this file

- **Loading.** Employment skills load this file at the step where they identify the governing jurisdiction as `ksa` and before any finding is written: `termination-review` (Step "identify governing law and contract type"), `hiring-review` (Step "statutory requirements checklist"), `worker-classification` (Step "apply the jurisdiction's test"), `wage-hour-qa` (Step "retrieve the statutory rule"), `policy-drafting` and `handbook-updates` (Step "statutory floor for each policy topic"), `log-leave` and the `leave-tracker` agent (Step "look up the leave type's statutory entitlement and deadlines"), `internal-investigation` (Step "procedural requirements"). Corporate skills `diligence-issue-extraction` and `material-contract-schedule` load it when reviewing employment contracts, and `integration-management` when planning a transfer of employees (Art. 18 row). Every finding must cite the row's article number and carry the row's tag verbatim.
- **termination-review** must cite, in order: Art. 74 (termination event), Art. 37 or Art. 55 (contract type), Art. 75 (notice: employer 60 days, worker 30 days for monthly-paid indefinite contracts; 30 days otherwise), Art. 76 (pay in lieu), Art. 80 (grounds for no-cost dismissal and their procedural conditions), Arts. 71-72 (hearing and grievance), Art. 77 (compensation), Arts. 84-88 (award and settlement timing), Art. 82, Art. 155 and Art. 234 (protected periods and limitation), and Art. 83 (post-termination restraints). It must not cite reinstatement under Art. 78; that remedy was repealed by M/46.
- **hiring-review** must cite Arts. 3 and 61(4) (non-discrimination), Arts. 33, 36, 38 (permit and reserved professions for non-Saudis), Art. 37 (non-Saudi contracts fixed-term, default one year), Art. 40 (employer bears fees), Arts. 51-52 (written, documented, model contract), Art. 53-54 (probation ≤ 180 days, once), Art. 61(5)-(6) (housing and transport), Art. 9 (Arabic governs), Arts. 161-167 (minors), and the Art. 7 exclusions.
- **worker-classification** must cite Art. 2 (worker definition), Art. 5(1) (subordination test), Art. 6 and the Art. 2 definitions of temporary, casual, seasonal and part-time work, Arts. 119-120 with HRSD Regs Art. 27 (part-time and flexible work), and Art. 7 (excluded categories). Assignment (الإسناد) under Art. 2 and Art. 30 is a licensed activity; a skill must flag unlicensed staff-supply arrangements against Art. 229 bis.
- **wage-hour-qa** must cite Art. 2 (wage definitions), Arts. 89-97 (payment, WPS, deductions), Arts. 98-108 (hours, Ramadan, breaks, weekly rest, overtime, exemptions) with the HRSD Regs Arts. 22, 22 bis and 23 points in the Practical effect column, and must state the overtime formula as "hourly wage plus 50% of hourly basic wage" and the 720-hour annual cap with its `[authority — HRSD]` tag.
- **policy-drafting** and **handbook-updates** must cite Arts. 12-13 (work regulations must follow the HRSD model and be approved), Arts. 66-73 (closed list of penalties and procedure), Arts. 109-118 and 151-160 (leave floors), Art. 83 (restraint limits), Art. 8 (floors cannot be lowered), and Art. 9 (Arabic version governs). Any policy provision below a row's floor must be flagged as void under Art. 8.
- **log-leave** and **leave-tracker** must map leave types to rows: annual (Arts. 109-111), official holidays (Art. 112 with Regs Art. 24 dates), marriage/bereavement/paternity (Art. 113: 5 days, 3 days sibling, 3 days birth within 7 days), Hajj (Art. 114), exams (Art. 115), unpaid (Art. 116, suspension after 20 days), sick (Art. 117: 30 / 60 at 75% / 30 unpaid per rolling year), maternity (Art. 151: 12 weeks, 6 mandatory after delivery, up to 4 before, plus 1 unpaid month, plus 1 paid month for a sick or disabled child), iddah (Art. 160: 4 months 10 days or 15 days), and must set decision-point alerts at: day 30 and day 90 of the sick-leave year (pay-tier changes), day 120 (Art. 82 termination bar lifts), day 180 of pregnancy-related absence (Art. 155 protection limit), and the end of the following year for deferred annual leave (Art. 110).

- **Stop rules.** If a skill needs a rule that has no row here (for example the Domestic Workers Regulation, the Social Insurance Law retirement age, the Wage Protection System decision, the violations schedule, Labor Court procedure, or a sector-specific hours decision under Art. 99), it must say that the rule is not in the reference file and stop or flag for Saudi counsel; it must not fill the gap from memory. If a skill detects that the contract or policy under review was signed before 19 February 2025 and the question turns on an M/44 change (Arts. 7, 37, 51, 53, 61(4)-(6), 72, 74(3 bis)/(7 bis), 75, 79 bis, 107(1), 113, 151), it must note that the pre-amendment text applied at signing and that the amended text now governs, and flag transitional treatment for counsel.
