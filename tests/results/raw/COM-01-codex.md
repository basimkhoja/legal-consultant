**F2 — Clause 3: Liability exclusion and cap**

**Playbook:** “Vendor cap at 12 months fees paid or payable”; indirect/consequential damages “excluded both ways”; required above-cap carveouts include gross negligence, fraud, confidentiality, and data breach.

**Contract:**
> “Vendor shall not be liable to Customer for any loss whatsoever arising out of or in connection with this Agreement, including loss caused by Vendor's gross negligence, and in no event shall Vendor's aggregate liability exceed SAR 100,000.”

**Gap:** Unacceptable. **Legal risk 🔴; business friction 🔴.**

The required dimensions are:

| Dimension | Assessment |
|---|---|
| Direct damages | “Any loss whatsoever” first attempts to exclude all recovery. The subsequent cap does not affirmatively restore direct-damages recovery. |
| Indirect/consequential damages | Vendor receives a blanket exclusion; Customer receives no reciprocal exclusion. Customer’s recoverable indirect exposure is not contractually capped or excluded. |
| Exact cap base | **“SAR 100,000.”** This is a fixed amount, not a fees-based cap. |
| Above/below cap | No express carveouts. On its wording, ordinary breach, confidentiality, data breach, and delay compensation all face the blanket exclusion and cap. No indemnity exists. |
| Mandatory limits | Art. 173 prohibits exclusion of fraud/gross fault and harmful-act liability. Its exception includes “غش أو خطأ جسيم”. The clause’s contrary reach cannot stand. |
| Playbook conflict | The profile says both “paid or payable” and “fees paid in the 12 months preceding the claim.” The latter can produce a very low early-term cap. The proposal below uses paid/payable with first-year protection. `[review]` |

The capped remedy is commercially illusory because the opening exclusion removes the very recovery the cap appears to preserve. Article 180 also distinguishes ordinarily foreseeable contractual loss from loss involving fraud or gross fault. [Civil Transactions reference, Arts. 173 and 180](</Users/basimkhoja/Projects/Legal Consultant/references/jurisdictions/ksa/civil-transactions-law.md:66>) `[settled — last confirmed 2026-09-04]` `[BOE — Arabic]`.

**F4 — Clause 2: Agreed compensation for delay**

**Accepted playbook default:** “Accept a fixed compensation clause only with the statutory reduction in view.”

**Contract:**
> “Vendor shall pay Customer a penalty of 10% of the total contract value per week of delay, payable irrespective of any loss suffered by Customer. The parties agree that this penalty is a genuine pre-estimate and is not subject to reduction by any court or tribunal.”

**Gap:** Unacceptable statutory-override wording; commercially excessive-looking formula. **Legal risk 🟠; business friction 🟠.**

**Enforceability `[ksa]`:** Art. 178 allows advance compensation for non-monetary obligations. Under Art. 179, no compensation is due if Vendor proves no harm; the court may reduce an exaggerated amount or account for partial performance. An increase requires Customer to prove greater harm resulting from fraud or gross fault. The article concludes:

> “يقع باطلًا كلُّ اتفاقٍ يُخالف أحكام هذه المادة.”

Accordingly, the “irrespective of loss” and “not subject to reduction” language is void to that extent. [Civil Transactions reference, Arts. 178–179](</Users/basimkhoja/Projects/Legal Consultant/references/jurisdictions/ksa/civil-transactions-law.md:68>) `[settled — last confirmed 2026-09-04]` `[BOE — Arabic]`.

**Exposure as written:** Assuming “total contract value” means the initial three-year fees and one delayed milestone, the formula is **SAR 540,000 per week**, or **SAR 1,080,000 after two weeks**, without an express clause 2 ceiling. `[computed — civil-transactions-law.md Arts. 178–179, settled 2026-09-04; inputs: agreement cls. 1, 2, 8; SAR 1,800,000 × 3 × 10% × 1 or 2 weeks]` `[review]`

Clause 3 nevertheless purports to eliminate or cap that recovery at SAR 100,000. The two clauses do not produce a reliable collectible amount.

**After the statutory rule:** The accrued agreed sum is ordinarily the maximum under Art. 179 absent the fraud/gross-fault condition for an increase; it remains reducible and may become zero on proof of no harm. No actual harm, delay duration, or performed share is supplied, so no judicially adjusted amount can be computed.

**Redline R4 — retain the milestone trigger, replace the remainder:**

> “Vendor shall pay Customer agreed compensation of 10% of the total fees for the initial three-year term per week of delay, subject to Article 179 of the Civil Transactions Law and the applicable aggregate cap in clause 3. No agreed compensation is due where Vendor proves that Customer suffered no harm; the court may reduce it for exaggeration or partial performance, and may increase it only on the grounds permitted by Article 179. This clause does not exclude Customer’s rights to require performance or terminate, and no loss shall be recovered twice.”

**العربية:**
> «يلتزم المورّد بأن يدفع للعميل تعويضاً اتفاقياً بنسبة 10% من إجمالي رسوم المدة الأولية البالغة ثلاث سنوات عن كل أسبوع تأخير، مع مراعاة المادة 179 من نظام المعاملات المدنية والحد الإجمالي المنطبق بموجب البند 3. ولا يستحق التعويض الاتفاقي إذا أثبت المورّد أن العميل لم يلحقه ضرر؛ وللمحكمة تخفيضه للمبالغة أو التنفيذ الجزئي، ولا تجوز زيادته إلا للأسباب التي تجيزها المادة 179. ولا يستبعد هذا البند حق العميل في طلب التنفيذ أو الفسخ، ولا يُعوض عن الضرر ذاته مرتين.»

**F5 — Clause 5: Six-month claims waiver**

**Contract:**
