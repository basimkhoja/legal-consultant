## Scope

The Law governs compulsory enforcement (التنفيذ الجبري) of enforcement instruments (السندات التنفيذية) by the enforcement courts: the court's jurisdiction and its limits, what counts as an enforcement instrument, the conditions for enforcing foreign judgments, awards, settlement agreements and notarised documents, the enforcement procedure (registration, order, service, disclosure of assets, compulsory measures, asset tracing, attachment, sale, distribution), direct enforcement of non-monetary obligations, personal-status enforcement, enforcement against private legal persons, enforcement disputes and grievances, criminal penalties for obstructing enforcement, and the licensing of private enforcement-service providers. It excludes execution of criminal penalties and matters within the jurisdiction of the Board of Grievances courts (Art. 4(1)), which have their own enforcement law (Royal Decree M/15 of 1443H). Precautionary attachment (الحجز التحفظي) and civil insolvency (الإعسار) are not in this Law: the corresponding chapters of the 1433H law continue to apply under Decree ¶6–¶7 until moved into the Law of Civil Procedure or replaced by a civil-insolvency statute. The Law is not yet in force on the date of reading; the 1433H law governs enforcement files until 2026-10-28.

## Open questions for a local practitioner

- **Pending files at 2026-10-28.** Decree ¶2–¶3 preserve prior orders and finally decided disputes; they do not say whether steps in a pending enforcement file after the effective date follow the new procedure (e.g. the new Art. 19 travel-ban caps, Art. 11 ten-year limit, Art. 18 fine). Confirm the Regulations' transitional article and any Supreme Judicial Council circular.
- **Implementing Regulations.** Not available on the reading date. Items that cannot be advised on without them: which creditors must give a pre-filing notice (Art. 12); the daily-fine caps (Arts. 18(3), 36(3)(b)); the "small amount" threshold that lifts a travel ban (Art. 19(3)(b)); the conditions for registering bills and notes on the national platforms (Art. 7(1)(d)); the definition of "obstruction" by a legal person's personnel (Art. 41); the list of grievable orders (Art. 46); the period to close a satisfied file (Art. 42); the bank-participation rules with SAMA (Art. 33(2)).
- **Precautionary attachment and insolvency** remain under the 1433H text (Decree ¶6–¶7); the text was not provided, so this folder cannot support a pre-judgment freezing analysis.
- **Foreign judgments.** Art. 9(1)(b) bars enforcement where a "similar case" was pending in the Kingdom before the foreign case was registered; how the enforcement court treats a Saudi case filed *after* the foreign case but before enforcement, and how reciprocity is proven for common-law states, is practice-dependent. `[model knowledge — verify]`
- **Foreign awards.** Art. 9(2) applies the Art. 9(1) conditions "in addition" to treaty obligations; whether the court reads this as adding grounds beyond New York Convention Art. V (e.g. the prior-Saudi-case ground) needs confirmation once the Law is applied. `[model knowledge — verify]`
- **Interest and Sharia public order.** The Law does not address the severance of interest components in foreign judgments or awards; practice under the 1433H law was to enforce the principal and refuse interest. `[model knowledge — verify]`
- **Art. 41 personal exposure of officers** of a debtor company (travel ban, imprisonment) depends entirely on the Regulations; until issued, advise only that exposure exists in principle.
- **Corporate guarantees and cheques.** Art. 7(1)(e) keeps cheques as enforcement instruments without a registration condition, unlike bills and notes; confirm whether the Regulations add conditions (e.g. bank certification of dishonour) before advising on cheque-based security.
