## Scope

The Law applies to every arbitration conducted in the Kingdom, whatever the nature of the legal relationship, and to international commercial arbitration conducted abroad where the parties agree to subject it to the Law, without prejudice to Islamic Sharia and the international conventions the Kingdom is party to (Art. 2). It does not apply to personal-status disputes or to matters not open to compromise. It covers the arbitration agreement (form, capacity, effect on court proceedings, separability), the tribunal (composition, qualifications, appointment, challenge, removal), interim measures by the court and the tribunal, the procedure, the applicable law, the award (time limit, form, deposit, interpretation and correction), the single remedy of annulment, and the enforcement order. The Regulations add the identity of the competent court, notification mechanics, appointment and challenge details, and the Supreme Court's role in appeals. The SCCA Rules are the institutional rules most often chosen for Saudi-seated arbitration; they bind only by agreement and yield to any mandatory provision of the applicable arbitration law (Rules Art. 2(4)). Enforcement of awards after the enforcement order, and enforcement of foreign awards, is governed by the Enforcement Law: see `enforcement-law.md` (Arts. 7(1)(b), 7(1)(g), 9(2)), cross-referenced here but not restated.

## Open questions for a local practitioner

- **Draft new Arbitration Law.** A draft replacement Arbitration Law was reported to have been released for public consultation in September 2025 (reported themes: relaxing arbitrator qualifications, institutional emergency measures, clearer treatment of government-entity arbitration). It was not in the source set; nothing above relies on it. Confirm its status and whether any provision has been enacted since the M/21 of 1447H amendment. `[model knowledge — verify]`
- **Government-entity arbitration (Art. 10(2)).** The in-force text still requires Prime Minister approval absent a specific statutory text. Which laws count as the "specific text" (e.g. Government Tenders and Procurement Law provisions, sector laws for MISA-licensed projects) and the current approval practice must be confirmed. `[model knowledge — verify]`
- **Arbitrator qualifications (Art. 14).** Whether a foreign law degree satisfies "شهادة جامعية في العلوم الشرعية أو النظامية", and how the courts treat awards by sole arbitrators without such a degree in international cases, is practice-dependent. `[model knowledge — verify]`
- **Interaction of institutional time limits with Art. 40.** Whether adopting institutional rules that permit extensions by the institution counts as the parties "agreeing a longer period" under Art. 40(2), or whether a court extension is still needed after 18 months, is not settled in the text. `[model knowledge — verify]`
- **Non-arbitrable commercial matters.** The Law excludes "matters not open to compromise"; which commercial or regulatory disputes (e.g. commercial-agency registration, employment, insolvency-related claims, competition penalties) the courts treat as non-arbitrable is not in the text. `[model knowledge — verify]`
- **Interest and public order.** The treatment of interest components in awards (severance under Art. 55(2)(b) versus full annulment under Art. 50(2)) is practice-dependent. `[model knowledge — verify]`
- **Regs Art. 7 deletion.** With the deposit requirement gone, whether any registration of arbitrator engagements or awards with the SCCA is required for ad hoc arbitrations should be confirmed; only the Art. 44 court deposit remains in the text.
- **Emergency-arbitrator orders.** No provision of the Law addresses court enforcement of an emergency arbitrator's order; whether the competent court will support it under Art. 22–23 or treat it as an interim award for Art. 39(5) purposes is untested in the text. `[model knowledge — verify]`
- **SCCA Rules currency.** The 2023 edition and fee schedule were read; confirm no later amendment (the SCCA reserves the right to revise fees).
