## Scope

The Law gives legal effect to electronic transactions, electronic records and electronic signatures in the public and private sectors (Art. 2), on a technology-neutral definition of "electronic signature" (Art. 1(14)), and layers on top of it a licensed certification regime (certification service providers licensed by the Commission, digital certificates supervised by the NCDC, Art. 15-22). It applies to all electronic transactions and signatures except (Art. 3) transactions relating to personal status (الأحوال الشخصية) and the issuance of deeds (صكوك) for dispositions of real property, unless the responsible authority allows those to be done electronically. The statute does not exclude powers of attorney, negotiable instruments or wills in terms; any such exclusion would come from the Regulation or from the sector regulator (Ministry of Justice for notarised instruments), which were not read. Nobody is compelled to transact electronically (Art. 4). The Law does not itself regulate consumer online sales (E-Commerce Law), nor evidence generally (the Evidence Law of 1443H, not read, contains its own chapter on digital evidence `[model knowledge — verify]`).

## Open questions for a local practitioner

- The Implementing Regulation (Art. 30) was not read. It sets the technical conditions under which a signature is "made in accordance with the Law" (Art. 14(2)), the retention and original-record conditions (Art. 6(3), 8), the dispatch and receipt rules (Art. 13), and the licensing and foreign-accreditation procedures (Art. 15, 17). Whether any additional instruments (powers of attorney, negotiable instruments, wills) are excluded by the Regulation must be checked there; the statute excludes only personal-status transactions and real-property deeds.
- Which foreign certificate issuers, if any, the NCDC has accredited under Art. 17, and whether a widely used foreign platform's certificates are among them.
- The current allocation of the NCDC's functions (Ministry, CST, or Digital Government Authority) and the current name of the Commission.
- How the Commercial Courts treat platform-signed contracts in practice: whether an audit-trail certificate is routinely accepted as meeting Art. 8, and whether courts require the platform provider to authenticate it.
- Interaction with the Evidence Law (1443H) chapter on digital evidence: which of the two instruments governs admissibility and weight when they differ.
- Whether the Ministry of Justice has issued permissions under Art. 3 for electronic real-property deeds (the Najiz practice suggests so) and the scope of those permissions.
