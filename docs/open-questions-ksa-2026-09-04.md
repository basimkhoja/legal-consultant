# Open questions for Saudi counsel — ksa reference layer (2026-09-04)
Consolidated from the "Open questions for a local practitioner" section of every instrument file in `references/jurisdictions/ksa/`. Each item is a rule the primary text did not settle, a point where practice is known to diverge from text, or a document that could not be read (scanned PDF, regulation not located). Take this list to Saudi counsel; when an item is resolved, record the answer in the instrument file and in the plugin verification log.

## Anti-Concealment Law (`anti-concealment-law.md`)

- Implementing Regulation: the Law delegates the good-faith carve-out for Art. 4 instruments, travel-ban conditions (Art. 8), deportation procedure (Art. 11), reporting and reward procedures (Art. 18), and the criteria for enforcement officers (Art. 6) to the Implementing Regulation, which was not in the source set. Confirm the current text and whether it has been amended since 2021.
- Who is "treated as a holder of" Saudi nationality for Art. 1 (GCC nationals, Premium Residency holders, and for which activities)? The Royal Decree's reference to the Premium Residency Center suggests a role, but the Law does not define it.
- Investment Law "registration" versus Anti-Concealment "licence or approval": confirm that MISA registration under the 2024 Investment Law (and legacy MISA licences still in force) is accepted by the Ministry of Commerce and the Criminal Court as the licence that takes a foreign investor outside Art. 2, and how the Ministry treats activity outside the scope of the registration.
- Status and outcome of the one-time correction mechanism under clause "Second" of the Royal Decree: the closing date of the extended window, what evidence of correction the Ministry issues, and whether a corrected establishment is treated as clean for later diligence.
- The amendment-watch lead 2.6 refers to a "ministerial committee for the national anti-concealment programme"; whether that programme has issued binding rules or only enforcement guidance is unknown.
- Application to professional companies and to activities licensed by sector regulators (health, education, engineering): the "economic activity" definition is broad enough to cover them, but sector licensing rules may interact with the Investment Law's Excluded Activities list.
- Whether the Criminal Court in practice exercises the Art. 12(1)(a) discretion not to dissolve the establishment where an innocent buyer has since acquired it.

## Bankruptcy Law (`bankruptcy-law.md`)

- **Ipso facto clauses in liquidation.** Art. 126 applies a list of financial-restructuring provisions to liquidation but omits Arts. 22-25. Does a termination-on-liquidation clause therefore operate in liquidation, or do courts treat the trustee's control under Art. 100 and the estate's interest as overriding it? Practice reports were not available to me.
- **Post-2021 amendments.** The portal records only M/89 of 1441H (Law) and Council of Ministers Decision 171 of 1443H (Regulations). Confirm with counsel that no later amendment, and no Bankruptcy Commission or Ministry of Justice rule, has changed the 180+180-day moratorium arithmetic or the claims-bar mechanics since then.
- **Cross-border rules.** The Minister of Commerce's Cross-Border Bankruptcy Procedures Rules (Regs Art. 97(1)) were not read. Confirm their number, date, whether recognition of a foreign main procedure stays Saudi enforcement, and whether reciprocity is required.
- **Small-debtor criteria and creditor-petition threshold.** Both are Bankruptcy Commission decisions (Art. 9(2)(ز), (ح)) not read for this file; the SAR figures must come from counsel.
- **Retention of title and unregistered security.** Whether a retention-of-title supplier is a "creditor secured by real security" (tier (a) of Art. 196) or an unsecured creditor with a 5-day reclaim window under Regs Art. 30 depends on the Movable Assets Security Law registration position; unresolved in the text.
- **Art. 26 exclusion for government contracts.** The exclusion names contracts "concluded by government entities" under the GTPL; whether contracts with state-owned companies outside the GTPL (for example stc) benefit from the exclusion is not addressed.
- **Post-opening supplier priority.** Regs Art. 78 ranks continuing-business expenses third *outside* liquidation; in liquidation Art. 196(هـ) ranks them fifth. Whether unpaid post-opening supplies from a rescue procedure retain any priority if the case converts to liquidation is not stated.
- **Sector regulations under Art. 229(2).** SAMA, CMA, CST and the Ministry of Health may have issued regulated-entity bankruptcy rules that displace parts of the Law; none were read.

## Civil Transactions Law (`civil-transactions-law.md`)

- **Adhesion review in B2B deals (Arts. 40, 96, 104(3)).** The text does not confine adhesion-contract review to consumers. Whether commercial courts apply Art. 96 to standard-form B2B SaaS/vendor terms, and how the Decree para. 6 "nature of the commercial transaction" carve-out is being used, needs confirmation from recent commercial court practice.
- **"Gross fault" (خطأ جسيم) in Arts. 173, 179, 180.** Undefined in the text. Ask counsel how courts have drawn the line (recklessness? serious negligence?) since 2024; the answer determines how much protection a liability cap gives.
- **Late-payment compensation and interest.** The text gives delay damages (Art. 171), bars pre-agreed compensation for money obligations (Art. 178) and voids loan increases (Art. 385), but says nothing about a general prohibition on interest. Ask counsel (a) whether courts award anything beyond proven loss for late payment, (b) whether "late payment charges" framed as administrative costs survive, and (c) how foreign-law interest awards are treated at enforcement.
- **Contractual claims-bar periods (Art. 305 vs Art. 344).** Art. 305 voids agreements shortening limitation; Art. 344 lets the seller extend the defect-warranty period. Whether a "claims must be notified within 12 months" clause in a services contract is a void limitation clause or a valid condition of the warranty is unresolved in the text.
- **No-set-off clauses (Art. 282).** The text voids waiver of set-off "before the right arises"; whether commercial courts nonetheless enforce no-set-off clauses in B2B contracts as an agreed payment mechanism is unknown.
- **Decennial liability.** Absent from the Law. Confirm with counsel what latent-defect regime applies to contractors and engineers (Saudi Building Code, Engineers' Council rules, Government Tenders and Procurement Law for public works) and whether any court has imported a 10-year rule via Art. 1.
- **Hardship in muqawala (Art. 471(3)) vs general hardship (Art. 97).** Art. 471(3) lacks Art. 97's non-derogation sentence. Whether a fixed-price construction/engineering contract can validly exclude Art. 471(3) rebalancing is open.
- **Automatic renewal of service contracts.** The Law regulates auto-renewal only for leases (Art. 440). There is no statutory notice period, cap, or consumer-style cancellation rule for auto-renewing B2B subscriptions in this Law; the E-Commerce Law and consumer-protection rules may apply to B2C. Confirm whether any 2024-2026 regulation (Ministry of Commerce) has addressed subscription auto-renewal.
- **Official English translation.** Not in the source set. When obtained, conform the English column and note any divergences from the Arabic in the Practical effect column.
- **Application to pre-Law contracts (Decree para. 5).** How often are counterparties succeeding in pleading a pre-2023 "judicial principle" (e.g. on lost profit or penalty clauses) to displace the Law for legacy contracts?
- **Governing-law and language clauses.** The text has no conflict-of-laws chapter. Whether Saudi courts give effect to a foreign governing-law clause in a contract performed in the Kingdom, and how a bilingual contract with an English-prevails clause is treated, are outside this Law and must be confirmed.

## Labor Law (`labor-law.md`)

- **Working hours discrepancy.** The Bureau of Experts English appendix lists M/46 amendments to Arts. 98, 99, 100 (9 hours / 45 hours; 7 / 35 in Ramadan) and 104 (two paid rest days including Friday), but the portal does not mark those articles as amended and the Arabic body reads 8 / 48, 6 / 36 and a single Friday rest day. This file follows the Arabic. Counsel should confirm whether those provisions were ever enacted (the writer believes they were not) and whether any ministerial decision under Art. 99 changes the position for particular sectors.
- **Art. 77 fractional years.** The compensation for terminating an indefinite contract without a legitimate reason is "fifteen days' wage for each year of the worker's service". The text does not say whether partial years are pro-rated (as Art. 84 expressly does for the award) or ignored. Court practice is needed before a skill computes a number for a worker with, say, 3 years and 7 months.
- **Art. 77 contractual compensation below the statutory formula.** Art. 77 opens "unless the contract includes a specific compensation". Whether a contractual figure lower than 15 days per year or lower than the two-month floor survives Art. 8 (conditions less favourable than the Law are void) is not settled by the text.
- **Which wage for Art. 77.** Art. 77 says "wage" (actual wage, Art. 2) but does not say "last wage" as Art. 84 does. Whether courts use the last wage or an average is a practice point.
- **Resignation from an indefinite contract and the Art. 85 fractions.** M/44 defines "resignation" as the written wish to terminate a **fixed-term** contract, and Art. 74 now lists resignation (3 bis) separately from termination at will in indefinite contracts (3). Whether a worker who terminates an indefinite contract on 30 days' notice under Art. 75(1)(a) still receives only the Art. 85 fraction of the end-of-service award, or the full award, is unresolved on the text; pre-M/44 practice applied the fractions to any worker-initiated termination.
- **Art. 79 bis and Art. 77(2).** The defined "resignation" is "without any conditions or restrictions" and the resigning worker "is entitled to all rights under this Law". Whether an employer can still claim Art. 77(2) compensation (wages for the remaining term) from a fixed-term worker who resigns and is deemed accepted by silence needs confirmation.
- **Art. 75 and "legitimate reason".** The Law does not define سبب مشروع. Whether redundancy, restructuring, poor performance without prior warnings, or loss of a client contract are accepted as legitimate reasons, and whether an employer terminating on 60 days' notice with a stated reason is still exposed to Art. 77 if the court rejects the reason, is court practice.
- **Art. 61(5)-(6) allowances.** No amount or benchmark for "appropriate" housing and transport allowances is given, and it is not stated whether an all-inclusive salary already covers them. Whether these allowances form part of the wage for end-of-service and compensation purposes (the text says they are paid "with the wage") is a practice point.
- **Retirement age.** Art. 74(4) now refers to "the retirement age under the Social Insurance Law". The Social Insurance Law and its 2024 amendments were not supplied; the applicable age (and any transitional schedule) must be taken from that instrument.
- **Art. 11 joint liability.** M/46 removed the joint-and-several liability of principal and contractor. Whether courts nevertheless reach the principal (for example under civil-law principles or the Regulations on assignment) is a practice point relevant to outsourcing reviews.
- **Non-Saudi transfer after 12 months (Regs Art. 14, item 21).** The Regulations allow a non-Saudi to move to another employer after 12 months from entry on 90 days' notice "with due regard to Art. 77". How Art. 77(2) compensation is applied in that case, and whether Nitaqat conditions restrict it, needs confirmation.
- **Dispute route details.** The mandatory amicable-settlement step, the Labor Courts' procedural rules, and the enforceability of arbitration clauses after the repeal of Art. 224 are outside the text supplied.
- **GOSI and work injuries.** Arts. 133-139 apply only where the GOSI occupational-hazards branch does not (Art. 132). Which categories of workers fall outside GOSI coverage should be confirmed before applying the Art. 137-138 amounts.
- **Domestic workers and the Art. 7 categories.** The separate HRSD regulations for domestic workers, agricultural workers and short-visit non-Saudis were not supplied; hiring reviews for those categories cannot rely on this file.
- **Regulations not read.** HRSD implementing provisions on Arts. 84-88 (award), 90 (wage-protection mechanics and exemptions), 109-111 (leave accounting), 151 (maternity documentation) and 74-77 do not appear in the April 2025 Implementing Regulations read for this file; ministerial decisions on those points (including the Wage Protection System decision and the violations-and-penalties schedules) were not supplied.
