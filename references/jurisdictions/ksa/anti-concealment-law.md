## Scope

The Law criminalises "tasattur": any agreement or arrangement by which a person enables a non-Saudi to practise, for the non-Saudi's own account, an economic activity in the Kingdom that the non-Saudi is not licensed to practise, using the licence, approval, commercial register, or name issued to the concealer (Arts. 2–3). "Economic activity" is drawn widely: any profit-seeking activity that requires an approval or licence, whether commercial, investment, service, professional, industrial, or agricultural (Art. 1). Both the enabler and the non-Saudi beneficiary commit a crime, as does anyone who knowingly incites, assists, or advises (Art. 3(c)), and a separate tier of administrative "violations" catches the mechanics of concealment even without a completed crime: granting a non-Saudi instruments of absolute disposal over an establishment, or running the business through a bank account that does not belong to it (Art. 4). Enforcement is split between the Ministry of Commerce (oversight, reports, detection, and an internal committee for Art. 4 violations), the Public Prosecution (investigation of crimes), and the Criminal Court (trial of crimes) (Art. 5).

The Law does not itself say who counts as a properly licensed foreign investor; that question is answered by the Investment Law (Royal Decree No. M/19 dated 16/1/1446H) and its registration regime, which this file cross-references. The Law does not cover the Implementing Regulation's detail on good-faith cases, travel bans, deportation procedure, or reward procedures, all of which it delegates to that Regulation (Arts. 4, 8, 11, 18).

## Open questions for a local practitioner

- Implementing Regulation: the Law delegates the good-faith carve-out for Art. 4 instruments, travel-ban conditions (Art. 8), deportation procedure (Art. 11), reporting and reward procedures (Art. 18), and the criteria for enforcement officers (Art. 6) to the Implementing Regulation, which was not in the source set. Confirm the current text and whether it has been amended since 2021.
- Who is "treated as a holder of" Saudi nationality for Art. 1 (GCC nationals, Premium Residency holders, and for which activities)? The Royal Decree's reference to the Premium Residency Center suggests a role, but the Law does not define it.
- Investment Law "registration" versus Anti-Concealment "licence or approval": confirm that MISA registration under the 2024 Investment Law (and legacy MISA licences still in force) is accepted by the Ministry of Commerce and the Criminal Court as the licence that takes a foreign investor outside Art. 2, and how the Ministry treats activity outside the scope of the registration.
- Status and outcome of the one-time correction mechanism under clause "Second" of the Royal Decree: the closing date of the extended window, what evidence of correction the Ministry issues, and whether a corrected establishment is treated as clean for later diligence.
- The amendment-watch lead 2.6 refers to a "ministerial committee for the national anti-concealment programme"; whether that programme has issued binding rules or only enforcement guidance is unknown.
- Application to professional companies and to activities licensed by sector regulators (health, education, engineering): the "economic activity" definition is broad enough to cover them, but sector licensing rules may interact with the Investment Law's Excluded Activities list.
- Whether the Criminal Court in practice exercises the Art. 12(1)(a) discretion not to dissolve the establishment where an innocent buyer has since acquired it.
