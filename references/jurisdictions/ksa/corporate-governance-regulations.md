## Scope

Both instruments apply only to joint stock companies whose shares are listed on the Saudi Exchange. The CGR sets board composition, independence, committee, conflict-of-interest, general-assembly, disclosure, and record-keeping rules; it is mandatory for Main Market companies except for provisions marked "guiding" (CGR Art. 2(b)), and for Parallel Market (Nomu) companies only six named provisions are mandatory and the rest are guiding (Art. 2(c)). The LJSC Regulation fills in the Companies Law for listed companies: director duties, board election and removal, director remuneration, virtual assemblies and e-voting, share buy-backs and pledges, pre-emptive rights, proxies, and the authorisation mechanics for related-party and competing business under Companies Law Art. 27.

Unlisted companies (LLCs, unlisted JSCs, SJSCs, partnerships) are outside both instruments; for them the Companies Law and the Ministry of Commerce's implementing regulations apply, and the Ministry may issue its own governance regulation for other companies (Companies Law Art. 274(2)). See `companies-law.md` for those rules; this file cross-references the Companies Law only where the CMA text is built on it (board circulation, general-assembly notice and quorum, Art. 27 conflicts, minutes, penalties). Continuing-disclosure rules for material developments and substantial shareholdings live in the CMA's Rules on the Offer of Securities and Continuing Obligations, which were not in the source set.

## Open questions for a local practitioner

- Arabic text: the two CMA regulations were read only in the CMA's English translations. Confirm every quoted threshold and the Arabic renderings against the Arabic originals on cma.org.sa before any counterparty-facing use, and confirm whether a CGR edition later than the 18/01/2023 amendment exists.
- LJSC Regulation 2026 amendment (Resolution 3-44-2026 of 30/03/2026): identify which articles it changed and its effective date; the text read carries no change-marking. The board-removal procedure in Art. 6(c)–(k) and the SPAC reference in the definitions are candidates.
- Board resolutions by circulation: Companies Law Art. 82 allows them for "urgent matters"; whether the CMA treats a circulated resolution as counting toward the CGR Art. 30(b) quarterly-meeting minimum, and how it polices the "urgent" qualifier, is a matter of practice.
- Quorum treatment of conflicted directors: CGR Art. 42 excludes the conflicted director's vote; whether the director still counts toward the Art. 30(d) attendance quorum is not stated.
- Continuing obligations: the immediate-announcement rules for material developments, the 1 % related-party announcement mechanics (CGR Art. 41(6)), and the 5 % substantial-shareholding notification are in the Rules on the Offer of Securities and Continuing Obligations, not read. Supply the current article numbers and timing.
- Interaction with the Ministry of Commerce's Implementing Regulations of the Companies Law (in the source set as `authority/moc-companies-implementing-regs-ar.txt` but outside this file's task) on general-assembly procedure, registration of extraordinary resolutions within 15 days (Companies Law Art. 93(5)), and any Ministry governance regulation for unlisted companies under Art. 274(2).
- Sector overlays: SAMA (banks, finance companies), the Insurance Authority, and other supervisors impose governance rules that apply in addition to the CGR (Art. 2(d)); which prevails on conflict is not addressed in the text.
- Parallel Market: confirm whether any CMA Board resolution since 2023 has made further CGR provisions mandatory for Nomu companies (Art. 2(c) leaves this open).
