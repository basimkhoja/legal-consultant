## Scope

The Rules require every company registered under the Companies Law, other than a listed joint stock company, to identify the natural person(s) who ultimately own or control it, to keep a UBO register at the company, to disclose the UBO data to the Ministry of Commerce, to confirm the data annually, and to update it within 15 days of any change. Covered forms are the general partnership, limited partnership, joint stock company, simplified joint stock company, limited liability company, and the foreign company operating in the Kingdom (branch). A listed joint stock company is outside the Rules (Rules Art. 3), and a subsidiary of a listed company that is itself subject to UBO disclosure requirements is exempt from the disclosure duty but not from the other duties (Rules Art. 7).

The Rules sit alongside, and do not replace, the AML/CFT customer-due-diligence regime that financial institutions and designated non-financial businesses apply; the guide expressly requires companies to make their UBO data available to those institutions on request. Non-compliance is sanctioned through the Companies Law's general penalty (Art. 262, fine up to SAR 500,000) and alternative measures (Art. 264), not through a penalty clause of the Rules' own.

## Open questions for a local practitioner

- Instrument identification: the guide cites Minister of Commerce Decision No. (99) dated 5/6/1447H; law-firm notes cite Decision No. 267 dated 26 November 2025. Confirm the decision number, its Gazette publication, and the effective date (claimed 4 January 2026) from mc.gov.sa or the Umm al-Qura Gazette.
- The Rules' own text was not read. Confirm the article numbering used above (Arts. 3, 4, and 7 as cited by the guide), the exact list of exemptions (in particular whether companies wholly owned by the state or a public body, and companies in liquidation or bankruptcy, remain exempt after the cancellation referred to in guide item 1.5), and any transitional deadline for companies that existed before the Rules took effect.
- Whether the "prescribed period" for first disclosure by a pre-existing company is, as the guide says for formerly exempt companies, the next annual-confirmation date, or a fixed grace period after the effective date.
- Filing platform: whether the "approved electronic services" are the Saudi Business Center portal, the Ministry's "Wathq" service, or both, and whether the Ministry issues a certificate or acknowledgement that can be produced in diligence.
- Treatment of trusts, foundations, endowments (أوقاف), and foreign entities with bearer or nominee structures in the chain, which the guide's examples do not address.
- Interaction with the AML Law's beneficial-owner definition (which the guide's introduction paraphrases) and with the Commercial Register Law of 2024: whether UBO data is shared with, or verified against, other registers.
- Whether the SAMA circular on bank verification of UBO data through "Wathq" (lead 2.4, March 2026) has been issued and what it requires of account holders.

## How skills should use this file

- `corporate-legal:entity-compliance` loads this file at the step where it builds the Saudi entity's compliance calendar. It must cite the annual-confirmation row (due on the CR-registration anniversary; may be filed 30 days early) and the change-notification row (15 days), and carry the Art. 262 penalty row when stating exposure. The anniversary date must be computed from the CR registration date supplied by the user, and the result tagged `[settled — last confirmed 2026-09-04] [authority — MoC]` for the rule and `[user-supplied]` for the date.
- `corporate-legal:diligence-issue-extraction` loads this file when the VDR contains a Saudi entity's corporate records. It must request the documents listed in the Diligence implication row, apply the tier-1 computation from the Rules Art. 4(1) row (multiply through the chain, aggregate per natural person, test 25 %), and then test tier 2 against the Rules Art. 4(2) row. Findings must cite the tier and the guide item. Any mismatch that points to a non-Saudi controller without investment registration must be cross-referenced to `anti-concealment-law.md`.
- `corporate-legal:tabular-review` uses the Register contents row as the column set when tabulating UBO registers across group entities, and the Scope of companies row to decide which entities need a row (listed JSCs excluded; branches of foreign companies included).
- `corporate-legal:closing-checklist` adds, for every Saudi entity in the deal: (1) pre-signing confirmation that the initial disclosure and latest annual confirmation are on file; (2) a post-closing item "file UBO update with the Ministry within 15 days of completion" for every entity whose UBO changes on closing; (3) nominee disclosures from any shareholder of record that is not the beneficial holder.
- `commercial-legal:review` and `nda-review` do not need this file unless the counterparty's ownership is a diligence point; in that case they cite the Definition row only and route to `corporate-legal`.
- Computed number: the tier-1 percentage. The formula is the product of the percentages down each chain, summed across chains and across direct holdings for the same natural person, and summed across persons acting by agreement or close family. The skill must show the chain it used and tag the result `[settled — last confirmed 2026-09-04] [authority — MoC]` for the method and `[computed from user-supplied cap table]` for the number.
- Stop rule: if a skill needs a row that is missing (for example, the treatment of a trust or foundation in the chain, the state-owned exemption, the transitional deadline for pre-existing companies, or the filing platform name) or that is tagged `[model knowledge — verify]`, it must say so, cite this file, and stop or flag for Saudi counsel. It must not infer the Rules' text from the guide beyond what the guide states, and must not promote a `[model knowledge — verify]` tag.
