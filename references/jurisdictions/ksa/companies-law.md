## Scope

The Companies Law governs every company incorporated in the Kingdom (Art. 3: a company incorporated under the Law is Saudi and must have its head office in the Kingdom) and, except for the incorporation provisions, foreign companies operating in the Kingdom through a branch or representative office (Arts. 235-241). It recognises five forms — general partnership, limited partnership, joint-stock company (JSC), simplified joint-stock company (SJSC) and limited liability company (LLC) (Art. 4) — plus non-profit companies (Part 7), professional companies (Part 8) and holding/subsidiary rules (Part 9), and it regulates transformation, merger and division (Part 10), dissolution and liquidation (Part 12) and criminal and administrative penalties (Part 13). It replaced the 2015 Companies Law (M/3 of 1437H) and the Professional Companies Law (M/17 of 1441H) (Art. 280). It does not cover listed-company market conduct (Capital Market Law and CMA regulations govern trading, disclosure and takeovers of listed JSCs; the CMA is the competent authority for them), foreign-investment licensing (Investment Law), commercial registration formalities (Commercial Register Law), insolvency (Bankruptcy Law) or beneficial-ownership reporting (Ministry rules under Art. 274(3)). The Implementing Regulations read for this file cover unlisted JSCs, LLCs, non-profit and professional companies and restructurings; the CMA issues separate regulations for listed JSCs.

## Open questions for a local practitioner

- Post-2022 amendments: the portal marks no article as amended and names no amending instrument, but the Bureau of Experts "recent updates" listing includes the Companies Law and the portal has a "versions" tab that was not opened. Confirm from the Umm al-Qura index whether any royal decree has amended the Law since M/132 (the brief mentioned a 1447/03/12H listing date, which on my reading belongs to the adjacent Expropriation Law entry).
- Implementing Regulations: confirm the decision number and date (Ministerial Decision 284 of 23/6/1444H) and whether the Ministry has amended or re-issued the Regulations since 2023 (the text read is the 1444H/2023 edition); several rows rely on Regs Arts. 3, 6-8, 10, 14-33, 62-69, 86-95.
- Board size: the Law fixes only a minimum of three directors for a JSC; confirm whether any Ministry rule imposes a maximum for unlisted JSCs (the CMA governance regulations do for listed companies).
- Chairman/CEO separation: Art. 78 as read does not prohibit combining the roles in an unlisted JSC; confirm whether the Ministry's corporate-governance regulation for unlisted companies (Art. 274(2)) exists and imposes separation or other board rules.
- LLC unanimity matters: Art. 172 requires unanimity only for raising nominal value and suspending pre-emption; confirm that no other provision (or Ministry model articles) reintroduces the 2015-Law unanimity for changing nationality or increasing partners' obligations, and whether Ministry model articles are mandatory in content.
- Losses rule consequences: confirm in practice whether the Ministry treats a failure to hold the Art. 182/132 assembly as grounds for administrative action beyond the Art. 261(h) offence, and whether partners face any residual liability (the 2015-Law joint liability is absent from the text read).
- Change of control of a corporate partner: Art. 178 speaks of assignment of the interest; confirm whether Saudi courts or the Ministry treat an indirect transfer as triggering statutory redemption.
- Enforceability of shareholders' agreements governed by foreign law or providing for foreign-seated arbitration against the registered Arabic articles (Art. 11), and the treatment of English-language SHAs in the Commercial Court.
- Practical notarisation: whether the Ministry's platform still requires Ministry of Justice electronic notarisation for LLC share transfers and amendments, and which cases fall within Regs Art. 2's "notarisation authority" category.
- Beneficial-ownership rules under Art. 274(3): the Ministry's UBO rules (thresholds, filing deadline after a transfer, sanctions) were not read.
- Language of minutes and registers: the Law is silent; confirm the Ministry's inspection practice on English-only minutes and whether Arabic minutes are required for registration of resolutions with the Commercial Register.
- Filing platform names and deadlines for the manager's/board's activity report to the Ministry (Regs Arts. 31, 65) and the financial-statements deposit (Regs Art. 5), and whether late-filing fines are being imposed in practice.
- SJSC and JSC interaction with CMA rules where the company is "seeking listing" (Arts. 111(1), 271): confirm at what point CMA rules displace the Ministry's Regulations.
